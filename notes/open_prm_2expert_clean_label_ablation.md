# Two-Open-PRM Clean-label Ablation

Date: 2026-09-05

This note records a robustness ablation for MoPRM: remove both OpenAI
judge-style experts and keep only the two non-OpenAI open-source reward
experts.

## Motivation

The main four-expert pool contains:

```text
open_math_prm
open_reasoning_rm
openai_general_judge
openai_reflective_judge
```

Although the pool already includes two non-OpenAI experts, the candidate
answers are generated by OpenAI and two of the four experts are OpenAI
judge-style scorers. This raises a reasonable concern:

> Is the Candidate Gate-v2 result mostly an artifact of OpenAI-generated
> candidates being judged by OpenAI-based experts?

This ablation does not remove OpenAI candidate generation. It specifically
tests whether the positive routing result still holds after removing the
OpenAI judge experts from the expert pool.

The retained experts are:

```text
open_math_prm       # Skywork-o1-Open-PRM-Qwen-2.5-1.5B
open_reasoning_rm   # Skywork-Reward-V2-Qwen3-1.7B
```

The removed experts are:

```text
openai_general_judge
openai_reflective_judge
```

## Complementarity

Top-choice success pattern legend:

```text

```


Interpretation:

- `open_reasoning_rm` carries most of the pool.
- `open_math_prm` is weak as a standalone selector but uniquely rescues two
  math problems where `open_reasoning_rm` selects a wrong candidate.
- There is one problem where both open-source experts choose a wrong top
  candidate.

average score on wrong candidates:


This explains why a simple single-expert baseline is already very strong:
`open_reasoning_rm` has much cleaner score separation than `open_math_prm`.

## Routing-relevant Subset

A more meaningful view is the subset where at least one of the two experts'
top choices is wrong:

```text
routing-relevant records: 28
```

All-84 trained Candidate Gate-v2 on success patterns:

```text
pattern 11: 56 / 56
pattern 01: 25 / 25
pattern 10:  2 / 2
pattern 00:  0 / 1
```

Overall:

```text
routing-relevant subset: 27 / 28 = 0.964
```

Interpretation:

- When only `open_reasoning_rm` has a correct top choice, Gate-v2 follows it.
- When only `open_math_prm` has a correct top choice, Gate-v2 successfully
  switches to it in both available cases.
- When neither expert's top choice is correct, Gate-v2 cannot recover.

## Takeaway

This ablation gives a stronger answer than the previous three-expert no-RM
analysis to the OpenAI-judge homogeneity concern.

The previous no-RM ablation removed `open_reasoning_rm` but kept two OpenAI
judge experts. This new ablation does the opposite: it removes both OpenAI
judge experts and keeps only the two open-source reward experts.

Result:

```text
two open-source experts only, clean labels:

clean mixed 67:
Candidate Gate-v2:   65 / 67
best single:         64 / 67
expert oracle:       66 / 67

all original 84:
Candidate Gate-v2:   83 / 84
best single:         81 / 84
expert oracle:       83 / 84
```

Therefore, Candidate Gate-v2's positive behavior does not depend on OpenAI
judge experts being present. Even with only two open-source PRM/RM experts,
V2 can exploit the small complementarity between `open_math_prm` and
`open_reasoning_rm`.

The result should still be presented with two caveats:

1. The candidate answers are still OpenAI-generated.
2. After label cleanup, the 84-problem subset is small and near ceiling.

For the final report, this is best framed as a robustness ablation:

> Removing all OpenAI judge experts does not eliminate the Candidate Gate-v2
> gain; a two-open-PRM pool still improves over the strongest open-source
> single expert and reaches the two-expert oracle on the clean-label all-84
> evaluation.
